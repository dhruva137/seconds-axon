# Seconds: the economics

Seconds is a market for food that is about to become waste. Everyone in it has to come out ahead, or they leave:

| Who | What they get | What they give |
|---|---|---|
| **Seller** (bakery, kitchen, shop) | Cash for food that was going in the bin; up to ₹12/kg dumping charge avoided; rescue credits; a donation register for FSSAI | 15% of what they sell; ₹3 a portion if it sells from a kirana chill hub |
| **Buyer** | Real food at 50–100% off, free in the last hour; 1 point per ₹10 (redeem later) | The price at that moment, ₹4 per paid order, the delivery fee if delivered |
| **Rider** | ₹30 + ₹7/km per run, +₹15 for each extra order on the same run; about ₹100 an hour on the road | Time |
| **Kirana chill hub** | ₹2 for every portion sold from its fridge, plus footfall | Fridge space |
| **CSR sponsor** | A verifiable, sealed record of every free meal (who, where, when, how much) | ₹8 per free portion (pilot assumption) |
| **Seconds** | Everything above minus what it pays out | Operations, support, servers |

## The rules that stop any order losing money

Every rule is in `server.js` and the same numbers are used by the simulator.

1. **Commission:** 15% of food sold.
2. **Delivery fee ≥ 1.1 × rider pay**, always: `fee = max(₹35, ₹27 + ₹8·km, 1.1 × (₹30 + ₹7·km))`. Delivery runs at cost or better, never below.
3. **Batching:** a second order to the same shop within 10 minutes joins the rider already heading there. The rider gets +₹15 and the buyer pays the full fee, so a batched order earns about ₹20 more than a solo one.
4. **Credits can cover at most half a seller's fee.** They are never cash, so they can never cost more than half of what that seller pays.
5. **Points cost under 1% of food.** 1 point per ₹10, worth ₹0.10, about 70% redeemed.
6. **Free food never pays a fee.** A free order is paid for by a CSR sponsor (₹8 a portion), or it costs nothing (pickup).
7. **Platform fee ₹4 on paid orders only.** Food apps charge ₹6–15.

So per order: `margin = 15%·food + ₹4 + (fee − rider pay) + hub share + CSR − points − credits used ≥ 0`. The server works this out for every finished order, and the admin dashboard shows it. The tests check that no order in the run loses money.

## The alpha: where the money really comes from

1. **A falling price (a Dutch auction on a clock).** Each buyer pays roughly what they are willing to pay: the eager ones early at ₹40, the careful ones later at ₹15, everyone else free in the last hour. Compared with a flat 50% off, the same food gets **63% more orders** and **waste falls from 58% to 33%**.
2. **Kirana chill hubs (inventory float).** A kirana fridge turns 2 hours of shelf life into 10. The price keeps falling from where it was, over a longer clock. Hubs add about **10% more orders** and **40% more profit per zone**, and the kirana earns ₹2 a portion for space it already has.
3. **Batching (route density).** Surplus clusters: one shop, one moment, many orders. The second order on a run costs ₹15 instead of ₹30+. Batching adds about **12% to profit per zone**.
4. **Supply that pays to leave.** Sellers pay up to ₹12/kg to dump food. Any sale beats that, so supply grows without paying for it.
5. **The fee floor.** Rain pushes rider pay up 30%. Without the floor, 10% of orders would lose money (down to −₹12.5 each); with it, none do.

## Stress test

`node econ/stress.js` simulates one city zone (about 5 km across) minute by minute from 10:00 to 03:00.

**Supply**
- **Listings:** Poisson, 45 a day. 55% cooked (after lunch and after dinner), 30% bakery (evening), 15% other.
- **Portions:** log-normal, median 10.
- **Normal price:** log-normal, median ₹70.
- **Start price:** half the normal price, falling to free in the last hour.

**Demand**
- **Arrivals:** a Poisson process with lunch, evening and late-night peaks; 350 buyers a day.
- **Willingness to pay:** each buyer's is Beta(2, 3) × the normal price.
- **Choice:** each buyer looks at up to 8 listings and takes the best surplus, including the delivery fee.
- **Pickup vs delivery:** 40% prefer delivery. Pickup is up to 2.5 km.

**Operations**
- **Riders:** 5–15 minutes to the shop, 18 km/h.
- **No-shows:** 7% on pickups.
- **Hubs:** a cooked listing still 40% unsold halfway through its clock moves to a fridge (3 hubs, 80 portions each) with 40% probability.

**Costs**
- **Fixed:** ₹1,800 a day per zone (operations, support, servers, SMS, marketing).
- **Seller plan:** ₹799/month, taken by 35% of 60 sellers.

### What it says

- **No order loses money in any scenario** while the fee floor is on.
- **The base case clears its fixed costs on about 98% of days**, about ₹600 a day per zone after costs. Across 50 zones in Bengaluru that is about ₹1.1 crore a year, before scaling fixed costs across zones.
- **Commission alone is not enough.** "Marketplace only" loses about ₹1,000 a day. The business is the stack: commission + ₹4 platform fee + CSR for free meals + the seller plan.
- **The weak spot is thin demand.** Halve the buyers and only about half of days break even; add stingy buyers and rain on top and most days lose a little. A zone needs about 110 orders a day to break even, so we open zones where offices, colleges and PGs are dense, and close or merge the rest.
- **Riders earn about ₹100 an hour on the road**, more in rain (pay rises 30%, and the fee floor passes it to the buyer).

### What is assumed, not known

The demand curve, willingness to pay, hub adoption, CSR rate and plan uptake are assumptions for a pilot to measure. The rules that keep each order positive do not depend on them.
